The filter carries two hidden states and no others: a level, which is where your trajectory sits today, and a velocity, which is how fast that level is moving per week. Every reading updates both, and the chart draws the level.

A reading pulls the level toward itself in proportion to how much noise the model expects from a scale. A single high morning therefore moves the line very little and the velocity state less still — which is the whole point of estimating a trajectory rather than plotting the measurements.

The interval beside the estimate is the posterior spread of the level. It widens when readings are sparse or irregular and narrows when they are dense, independently of the direction the level is moving. Forward of today it widens further at every step, because the forecast compounds the uncertainty in the velocity state as well as the level: {{ widenNote }}

Parameters are fixed and documented rather than fitted to this history — nothing here is estimated per person, and the model is not offered as a universal winner. Read the Method for the derivation.
